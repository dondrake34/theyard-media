## Claims and sources (2+ each)
1. Night of Feb 8, 1968, S.C. highway patrolmen fired on unarmed Black student protesters on the South Carolina State College
   campus in Orangeburg; three killed.
   - HISTORY.com, Orangeburg Massacre: https://www.history.com/articles/orangeburg-massacre
   - Smithsonian Magazine (2018): https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/1968-three-students-were-killed-police-today-few-remember-orangeburg-massacre-180968092/
   - EJI calendar, Feb 8: https://calendar.eji.org/racial-injustice/feb/8 ; College of Charleston LDHI exhibit: https://ldhi.library.cofc.edu/exhibits/show/orangeburg-massacre/oburg-massacre-page
2. Protests began at the segregated All Star Bowling Lanes; SC State and Claflin students; arrests at a sit-in two days before.
   - NPS, South Carolina All Star Bowling Lane: https://www.nps.gov/places/south-carolina-all-star-bowling-lane.htm
   - CBS News: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/once-segregated-bowling-alley-south-carolina-community-preserving-history-reclaiming-it/
   - Wikipedia, Orangeburg Massacre (cross-check)
3. Students lit a bonfire on campus; officers came to put it out; patrolmen then fired.
   - Smithsonian (above); HISTORY.com (above); LDHI (above)
4. Killed: Samuel Hammond Jr. and Henry Smith (SC State students) and Delano Middleton (high school student whose mother worked at
   SC State). Hammond was shot in the back.
   - PBS Frontline, Delano Middleton: https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/interactive/unresolved/cases/delano-middleton
   - Smithsonian; HISTORY.com; BlackPast: https://www.blackpast.org/aah/orangeburg-massacre-1968
   - Not used: ages (PBS 17 vs. "all 18" in Smithsonian).
5. Wounded: 27 (Smithsonian) vs. 28 (HISTORY.com, others), so narration says "nearly thirty".
6. Nine patrolmen were charged federally and acquitted (1969 trial).
   - Smithsonian (above); Wikipedia (Orangeburg Massacre); EJI (no one held accountable)
7. Cleveland Sellers, wounded that night, was the only person convicted/jailed (inciting a riot, tied to the bowling alley
   events); pardoned by the S.C. Probation, Pardon and Parole Board in 1993.
   - Wikipedia, Cleveland Sellers; Democracy Now (2008): https://democracynow.org/2008/4/3/1968_forty_years_later_a_look
   - NPR (2018): https://www.npr.org/2018/02/10/584757736/50-years-since-the-orangeburg-massacre (wounded, conviction)
   - LDHI aftermath: https://ldhi.library.cofc.edu/exhibits/show/orangeburg-massacre/massacreaftermath-page
8. Gov. Jim Hodges apologized on behalf of the state in 2001.
   - HISTORY.com; Smithsonian; Wikipedia
9. "Two years before Kent State": Orangeburg Feb 1968, Kent State May 4, 1970 (both widely documented; Kent State University
   site compares them).
