On February eighth, nineteen sixty-eight, state highway patrolmen opened fire on unarmed students at an H. B. C. U. in South Carolina. Three young men never went home.

It started at a bowling alley. In Orangeburg, the All Star Bowling Lanes turned Black customers away. Students from South Carolina State and Claflin went there to protest, and some were arrested.

Two nights later, students gathered on the South Carolina State campus and lit a bonfire. Officers came to put it out. Then patrolmen fired into the crowd.

Samuel Hammond and Henry Smith were South Carolina State students. Delano Middleton was a high school student whose mother worked on campus. Hammond was shot in the back. Nearly thirty others were wounded.

Here's the part nobody tells you. Nine patrolmen were charged in federal court, and a jury acquitted them all. The only person sent to prison was Cleveland Sellers, an activist wounded that night, convicted of inciting a riot. The state pardoned him in nineteen ninety-three.

In two thousand one, Governor Jim Hodges apologized on behalf of South Carolina. It happened two years before Kent State, yet few people know these names.

Say them: Samuel Hammond, Henry Smith, Delano Middleton. Which H. B. C. U. story should we tell next?
