**Description**
In 1904, Mary McLeod Bethune opened a school in a rented house next to the Daytona Beach city dump. She had $1.50, five little girls, and her son Albert. Desks were crates. Ink came from elderberries. Pencils were slivers of burned wood.

That school became Bethune-Cookman University, and Bethune ended up advising a president and standing in the U.S. Capitol.
