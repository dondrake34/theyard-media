The oldest H. B. C. U. in America didn't start with a campus. It started with a will, and ten thousand dollars.

Its author was Richard Humphreys, a Quaker in Philadelphia. He left ten thousand dollars, about one tenth of his estate, to start a school for Black youth.

His will asked for a school to teach trades and farming, and to train Black teachers. In eighteen thirty-seven, it was founded. Soon it was called the Institute for Colored Youth.

But here's the part most people never hear. In eighteen sixty-nine, the school got a new head principal, Fanny Jackson Coppin. She was born enslaved. Her aunt bought her freedom, and she went on to graduate from Oberlin College.

She led the school for more than thirty years, training teachers for Black classrooms. Today, Coppin State University in Baltimore carries her name.

In the early nineteen hundreds, the school moved to a farm west of Philadelphia. It belonged to George Cheyney. That's where the name comes from: Cheyney University of Pennsylvania.
