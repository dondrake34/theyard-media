Nineteen H. B. C. U. schools are land-grant universities. It all traces back to a law from eighteen ninety. But the story starts in eighteen sixty-two.

That year, President Abraham Lincoln signed the Morrill Act. States got federal land to sell, and the money built colleges for farming and the mechanic arts.

But across the South, most of those colleges shut Black students out. The people who had worked the land the longest couldn't study there.

So Justin Morrill of Vermont came back with a second law, signed August thirtieth, eighteen ninety. No federal money for a state's college if it turned students away by race. Unless the state set up a separate college for Black students.

That's how the eighteen-ninety universities were born. Today there are nineteen, from Alabama A and M to Virginia State, and even Tuskegee.

But here's the part most people never hear. The law said the money had to be divided equitably. In twenty twenty-three, two U.S. cabinet secretaries told sixteen governors their land-grant H. B. C. U. schools had been shortchanged. A gap of more than twelve billion dollars, from nineteen eighty-seven to twenty twenty.
