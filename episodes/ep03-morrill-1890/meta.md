# Ep. 3 — Why 19 HBCUs Are Land-Grant Universities (the Morrill Acts of 1862 and 1890)

Standalone Short, made 2026-10-31, posts 2026-10-31 6:00 PM CT.

## Fact check (two independent sources per claim)
| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| 19 HBCU land-grant ("1890") universities today, incl. Tuskegee, Alabama A&M, Virginia State | APLU Council of 1890s Institutions (member list of 19); Alabama A&M "Morrill Act 1890"; Alabama Extension (ACES) "1890 Land-Grant Institutions" |
| First Morrill Act signed by President Lincoln on July 2, 1862; federal land sold to fund colleges for agriculture and the mechanic arts | U.S. Senate "Morrill Land Grant College Act" featured document; Iowa State University "Morrill Act of 1862" |
| Black students largely excluded from those colleges in Southern states; "people who had labored on the land the longest" | Virginia State University Cooperative Extension ("racism in southern and border states prevented African Americans" from benefiting); Diverse: Issues in Higher Education (2025) |
| Justin Morrill of Vermont; second Morrill Act approved Aug. 30, 1890 | U.S. Senate (Morrill, Vermont); Mississippi State DAFVM (act text, approved Aug. 30, 1890, 26 Stat. 417); Alabama A&M; VSU ("Morrill's 1890 legislation") |
| No funds to a state's college that makes "a distinction of race or color" in admission, unless separate colleges for white and Black students (funds "equitably divided") | Act text via Mississippi State DAFVM; Alabama A&M; Diverse |
| Sept. 18, 2023: Education and Agriculture secretaries wrote to 16 governors; >$12 billion state funding disparity, 1987–2020, vs. each state's 1862 land-grant | USDA press release (Sept. 18, 2023); The 74 (Sept. 2023); JBHE |

Wording choices: "most of those colleges" (some states designated Black schools under the 1862 act, e.g. Alcorn 1871); "shortchanged" / "gap" rather than claiming a specific per-school figure. No politician names beyond Lincoln/Morrill; the 2023 letter is stated factually without partisan framing.
