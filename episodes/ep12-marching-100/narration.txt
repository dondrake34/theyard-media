In nineteen forty-one, a young Black musician graduated from the University of Kansas. The school's marching band had kept him out because of the color of his skin.

His name was William P. Foster. He was told there would be no jobs for Black conductors in America. So he set out to build his own band.

In nineteen forty-six, Florida A and M hired him as its director of bands. He found just sixteen members.

Here's the part nobody tells you. Foster turned those sixteen into the Marching One Hundred, and the band grew to hundreds of players, famous for high steps and big sound.

In nineteen eighty-nine, France celebrated two hundred years since its revolution. For the Bastille Day parade in Paris, the Marching One Hundred was chosen to represent the United States.

In two thousand seven, the band played the Super Bowl halftime show in Miami. Foster led it for fifty-two years, until nineteen ninety-eight.
