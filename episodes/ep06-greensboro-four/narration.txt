Four college freshmen sat down at a lunch counter, asked to be served, and refused to leave. That one afternoon helped change the country.

They were students at North Carolina A. and T., an H. B. C. U. in Greensboro. Their names were Ezell Blair Junior, David Richmond, Franklin McCain, and Joseph McNeil.

On February first, nineteen sixty, they walked into the Woolworth's store downtown. The lunch counter there served white customers only. Black customers were not welcome to sit.

The four sat down anyway and politely asked for service. They were refused. But they did not argue, and they did not leave. They stayed in their seats until the store closed.

The next day, more students from local colleges came to join them. Within days, sit-ins were popping up in other North Carolina cities. Soon they spread to other states.

But here's the part most people never hear. It all began with four young men who simply stayed seated, calm and peaceful, while the whole country watched. On July twenty-fifth, nineteen sixty, that same Greensboro lunch counter finally served Black customers.
