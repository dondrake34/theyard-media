April eleventh, eighteen eighty-one. Eleven students walked down into a church basement in Atlanta. That basement became Spelman College.

Their teachers were Sophia Packard and Harriet Giles, two Baptist missionaries from New England. They came south to teach Black women and girls. They had almost no money.

Frank Quarles, pastor of Friendship Baptist Church, offered them his basement. The first class was ten women and one girl. Some of those women had been born enslaved.

Within months, the school had outgrown the basement. In eighteen eighty-two, the founders went north to raise money. In Cleveland, they met John D. Rockefeller.

Here's the part nobody tells you. In eighteen eighty-four, Rockefeller visited the school. He paid off the debt on its new campus, land that had once held Union army barracks.

The school was renamed Spelman Seminary, after his wife, Laura Spelman Rockefeller, and her parents, who had fought slavery. In nineteen oh one, Spelman awarded its first college degrees. In nineteen twenty-four, it became Spelman College.
