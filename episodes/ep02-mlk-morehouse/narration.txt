September, nineteen forty-four. A fifteen-year-old walks onto a college campus in Atlanta. He hasn't even finished high school. His name? Martin Luther King Junior.

The school was Morehouse College, an H. B. C. U. With so many young men away at war, Morehouse let younger students in early. His father and his grandfather had both studied there.

At first, he didn't want to be a preacher. He thought he could help people more as a lawyer, or a doctor. Then he met the college president, Benjamin Mays.

Every week, Mays spoke in the chapel. He told students to be sensitive to the wrongs and injustices of society. And he introduced young Martin to the ideas of Gandhi. Nonviolence.

At Morehouse, Martin first read Thoreau's essay on civil disobedience. In his senior year, he chose the ministry. And in nineteen forty-eight, at just nineteen, he graduated.

But here's the part most people never hear. Twenty years later, in nineteen sixty-eight, the final service of Martin's funeral was held at Morehouse. And the man who gave the eulogy? Benjamin Mays.
