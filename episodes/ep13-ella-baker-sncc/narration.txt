In nineteen sixty, at an H. B. C. U. in Raleigh, one woman helped student protesters keep their movement their own, even when Doctor King hoped to bring them into his group.

Her name was Ella Baker. In nineteen twenty-seven, she graduated from Shaw University at the top of her class.

That February, four freshmen sat down at a whites-only lunch counter in Greensboro, and sit-ins spread across the South. Baker was working for Doctor King's civil rights group, and she saw a movement being born.

She got the group to put up about eight hundred dollars, then invited the sit-in leaders to her old campus, Shaw University, on Easter weekend, April fifteenth to seventeenth.

Here's the part nobody tells you. Doctor King hoped the students would become a youth branch of his group. Baker urged them to stay independent and run their own organization.

They did. That weekend, they formed the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee. It joined the Freedom Rides in nineteen sixty-one, and it helped lead voter registration in Mississippi, including Freedom Summer in nineteen sixty-four.
