The law school in his hometown wouldn't take Black students. So Thurgood Marshall went to an H. B. C. U., and came back to beat that school in court.

Marshall grew up in Baltimore. In nineteen thirty, he graduated from Lincoln University in Pennsylvania, an H. B. C. U. founded before the Civil War.

The University of Maryland's law school, close to home, was closed to Black students. So he went to Howard University's law school in Washington.

At Howard, his mentor was the dean, Charles Hamilton Houston. Houston taught his students to use the law to change society. In nineteen thirty-three, Marshall graduated first in his class.

But here's the part most people never hear. In nineteen thirty-five, Marshall and Houston took that same Maryland law school to court, for a Black student named Donald Murray. They won, and Murray was admitted.

In nineteen fifty-four, Marshall won Brown versus Board of Education, and the Supreme Court ruled segregated public schools unconstitutional. In nineteen sixty-seven, he became the first Black justice on that same Court.
