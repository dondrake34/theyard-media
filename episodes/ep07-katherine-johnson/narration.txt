Before America trusted a computer with an astronaut's life, the astronaut asked for one woman to check the math. She learned that math at an H. B. C. U.

Her name was Katherine Johnson. At West Virginia State, a math professor named W. W. Claytor saw her gift, and he added new math courses just for her.

In nineteen thirty-seven, at just eighteen years old, she graduated with the highest honors, with degrees in mathematics and French.

In nineteen fifty-three, she joined the Langley lab in Virginia as a human computer, in the all-Black West Area Computing unit. She worked out the flight path for Alan Shepard, the first American in space, in nineteen sixty-one.

But here's the part most people never hear. For John Glenn's flight around the Earth in nineteen sixty-two, NASA had new electronic computers. Glenn still wanted Katherine to check their numbers herself. If she said they were good, he was ready to go. She checked them, and he circled the Earth three times.

In twenty fifteen, she received the Presidential Medal of Freedom. She lived to be one hundred and one.
