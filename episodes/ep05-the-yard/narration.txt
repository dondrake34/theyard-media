At most colleges, the big lawn in the middle of campus is called the quad. But on many H. B. C. U. campuses, it goes by another name. The Yard.

The Yard is where students meet between classes, catch up with friends, and see everybody they know. And at Howard University, in Washington, D. C., the Yard is the heart of campus.

On one side of Howard's Yard stands Founders Library. It was finished in nineteen thirty-nine and modeled on Independence Hall in Philadelphia.

But here's the part most people never hear. Howard's law school was once housed in that library. Inside, lawyers like Thurgood Marshall worked on the legal strategy behind Brown versus Board of Education, the case that struck down segregated public schools.

In two thousand one, the Yard and three of its buildings became a National Historic Landmark.

The Yard is also where the culture lives. Five of the Divine Nine, the Black Greek-letter organizations, were founded at Howard, and their letters still mark the Yard. Howard's homecoming goes back to nineteen twenty-four, and every fall, Yardfest brings thousands back home.
