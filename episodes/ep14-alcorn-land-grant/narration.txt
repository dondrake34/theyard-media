In eighteen seventy, the first Black man sworn into the United States Senate came from Mississippi. A year later, he left Washington to lead a brand-new H. B. C. U.

His name was Hiram Revels, a minister and teacher. He took the oath on February twenty-fifth, eighteen seventy, after opponents spent days trying to keep him out.

Back home, Mississippi bought Oakland College, a Presbyterian school closed by the Civil War. In eighteen seventy-one, the campus became Alcorn University, a college for Black students, named for Governor James Alcorn.

When his Senate term ended in eighteen seventy-one, Revels became Alcorn's first president.

Here's the part nobody tells you. Mississippi gave Alcorn a share of its federal land-grant money from the Morrill Act. That makes Alcorn the oldest public Black land-grant college in America, nearly twenty years before the law of eighteen ninety brought most of the others.

It became Alcorn A and M College in eighteen seventy-eight, and Alcorn State University in nineteen seventy-four. Medgar Evers studied there, then became the N. A. A. C. P.'s first field secretary in Mississippi.
