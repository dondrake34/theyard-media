## Claims and sources (2+ each)
1. Hiram Revels (Mississippi) was the first African American U.S. senator; took the oath Feb 25, 1870, after Democrats
   delayed his seating for days (citizenship challenge; seated 48-8).
   - U.S. Senate, "First African American Senator": https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/senate-stories/First-African-American-Senator.htm
   - NPS, Hiram Rhodes Revels: https://www.nps.gov/people/hiram-rhodes-revels.htm
   - Library of Congress blog: https://blogs.loc.gov/headlinesandheroes/2020/02/hiram-revels-first-african-american-senator/
   - HISTORY.com, Feb 25: https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/february-25/african-american-congressman-sworn-in
   - Not used: "Jefferson Davis's seat" (NPS says he filled Albert Brown's seat; popular accounts differ).
2. Revels was a minister (AME) and educator. Britannica: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Hiram-R-Revels ; NPS (above).
3. Mississippi bought the campus of Oakland College, a Presbyterian school closed by the Civil War; Alcorn University
   created 1871 for Black students, named for Gov. James L. Alcorn.
   - NPS National Register nomination, Alcorn State University Historic District: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/bdb21bc0-7b2e-4829-9fb8-5cff8cde0987
   - Britannica, Alcorn State University: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Alcorn-State-University
   - Alcorn State, History: https://www.alcorn.edu/discover-alcorn/history ; BlackPast: https://www.blackpast.org/african-american-history/alcorn-state-university-1871/
   - Price not used: $40,000 (Alcorn, AJC) vs. $42,500 (state marker) disagree.
4. Revels's Senate term ended in 1871 and he became Alcorn's first president.
   - Britannica (Revels); Library of Congress blog (above); Alcorn State, "founding president" (alcorn.edu, 2020-02-24 article).
   - Narration says "when his Senate term ended", not "resigned" (sources differ).
5. Alcorn received a share of Mississippi's federal land-grant funds from the (1862) Morrill Act, making it the oldest
   public historically Black land-grant institution; most other Black land-grant schools came with the 1890 Act.
   - Alcorn State, History ("America's oldest public historically black land-grant institution")
   - BlackPast, Alcorn State University (first Black land-grant institution funded under the 1862 Morrill Act)
   - 1890 Act and the 19 HBCU land-grants: sourced in ep03 meta.md (USDA NIFA, APLU).
   - Britannica dates the formal land-grant designation to 1878 with the A&M renaming; narration avoids a designation date.
6. Renamed Alcorn Agricultural and Mechanical College in 1878; Alcorn State University in 1974.
   - Britannica (Alcorn State); Wikipedia / Alcorn State history; ms.gov state profile.
7. Medgar Evers studied at Alcorn (graduation year differs: 1950 Britannica print vs. 1952 others, so no year given),
   then became the NAACP's first field secretary in Mississippi (1954).
   - Britannica, Medgar Evers: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Medgar-Evers
   - NPS, Medgar Evers: https://www.nps.gov/people/medgar-evers.htm ; Alcorn State (alumnus statue/article)
