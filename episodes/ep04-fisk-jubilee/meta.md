## Fact check (two independent sources per claim)
| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| In 1871 Fisk University (Nashville) was deep in debt and at risk of closing | Fisk University history page ("debt-ridden", hoping "to keep the school open"); Tennessee Encyclopedia ("impending closure"); Britannica ("severely in debt"); hmdb marker (NashVitality) |
| Classes were held in old Union Army barracks in poor condition | Fisk University history (former Union Army barracks); Tennessee Encyclopedia ("decaying barracks of a former Union army hospital") |
| George L. White, the school's treasurer, organized and led the group | Britannica (treasurer and music professor); Tennessee Encyclopedia (university treasurer); TSU Library |
| Left Nashville on October 6, 1871; nine student singers | Fisk University history (Oct. 6, 1871); PBS American Experience timeline (Oct. 6, 1871); nine members: Tennessee Encyclopedia, Britannica, NEA, Smithsonian Libraries |
| Many were born into slavery | TSU Library ("former slaves or children of slaves"); Wikipedia (cited) — wording kept to "many" |
| Small crowds at first; White named them the Jubilee Singers after the biblical year of jubilee (Leviticus 25), when the enslaved were freed | hmdb marker (small crowds, meager donations; White named them); Tennessee Encyclopedia (Leviticus 25:8-17); Britannica; TSU Library |
| They sang spirituals | TSU Library; NEA ("slave songs"); Tennessee Encyclopedia |
| Sang for President Ulysses S. Grant at the White House in 1872 | NEA National Medal of Arts page; PBS American Experience timeline (March 5, 1872) |
| Sang for Queen Victoria in England in 1873 | Britannica (1873 European tour); Tennessee Encyclopedia (1873 British tour); NEA |
| Money raised built Jubilee Hall, Fisk's first permanent building; still standing; National Historic Landmark | Fisk University history; Tennessee Encyclopedia (Jubilee Hall entry, NHL); NEA; hmdb marker |
| Fisk marks October 6 every year (Jubilee Day) | hmdb marker ("Jubilee Day is still celebrated annually on October 6"); Fisk University history (observes Oct. 6 each year); Diverse Issues in Higher Education (Jubilee Day) |

Left out (sources disagree or single source): total dollars raised ($40K/$50K/$150K vary by tour and source), who commissioned the Havell portrait, the Chicago Fire donation, the 1865 vs. 1866 founding date, "first permanent building in the country for Black education" (Fisk says "the South's first").
