In eighteen seventy-one, an H. B. C. U. was about to close its doors. Nine students saved it with nothing but their voices.

The school was Fisk University, in Nashville, Tennessee. It was deep in debt, and classes were held in old Union Army barracks that were falling apart.

So the school's treasurer, George White, took a gamble. On October sixth, eighteen seventy-one, he led nine student singers out on the road. Many of them had been born into slavery.

At first, the crowds were small. So White gave them a new name: the Jubilee Singers, after the Bible's year of jubilee, when the enslaved were set free. And they sang the spirituals they grew up with.

It worked. In eighteen seventy-two, they sang for President Ulysses S. Grant at the White House. The next year, in England, they sang for Queen Victoria.

But here's the part most people never hear. The money they raised built Jubilee Hall, Fisk's first permanent building. It still stands today, a National Historic Landmark. And every October sixth, Fisk celebrates Jubilee Day.
