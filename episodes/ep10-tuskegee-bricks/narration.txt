Three times, the brick kilns at Tuskegee failed. So Booker T. Washington pawned his own watch to try again.

Alabama lawmakers had set aside two thousand dollars for a new Black school in Tuskegee. It could only pay teachers. Nothing for land or buildings.

Lewis Adams, a man who had been enslaved, helped push for it. They hired Booker T. Washington, a young teacher from Hampton, who was born enslaved too.

On July fourth, eighteen eighty-one, Washington opened the school with about thirty students, in a shanty beside a church. When it rained, a student held an umbrella over him while he taught.

The students would make their own bricks. Washington wrote that the first kiln failed. So did the second. The third collapsed in the night.

But here's the part nobody tells you. With no money left, he pawned his watch in Montgomery for fifteen dollars. The fourth try worked. He never saw that watch again.
