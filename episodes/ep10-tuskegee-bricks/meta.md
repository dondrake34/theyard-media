## Fact check (two sources per claim; page fetches blocked in this session, sources found via web search)
| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| Alabama legislation (bill passed 1880–81) provided $2,000 for a Black normal school at Tuskegee; it covered teachers' salaries only, nothing for land, buildings or equipment | Tuskegee University "History and Mission"; NPS "Tuskegee Institute's Founding" (tuin); Tuskegee archive BTW biography; Wikipedia "Tuskegee University" |
| Lewis Adams, formerly enslaved, pushed for the school (with George W. Campbell, a former slaveholder) | Wikipedia "Lewis Adams"; NPS tuin founding page; National Archives "Rediscovering Black History: Happy Founder's Day Tuskegee University" |
| They hired Booker T. Washington, recommended by Hampton's principal Samuel Armstrong; Washington was a Hampton graduate and teacher | NPS tuin founding page; Tuskegee University "Dr. Booker Taliaferro Washington"; BhamWiki "Tuskegee Institute" |
| Washington was born enslaved | NPS "Booker T. Washington" (tuin); Britannica "Booker T. Washington"; Encyclopedia of Alabama |
| Opened July 4, 1881 with about 30 students in a shanty by Butler Chapel A.M.E. Zion Church | Tuskegee University "History and Mission" (30 adults, one-room shanty near Butler Chapel); NPS tuin ("first class of 30 male and female students in a shanty on the grounds of a church") |
| A student held an umbrella over Washington while he taught on rainy days | NPR "The Legacy of Booker T. Washington Revisited" (2015); Washington, *Up From Slavery*, ch. 8 |
| Students made their own bricks; first kiln (~25,000 bricks) failed, second failed, third collapsed at night | Washington, *Up From Slavery*, ch. 10 (narration attributes it: "Washington wrote"); NPS museum exhibit "Building a University: making bricks"; LitCharts ch. 10 summary |
| He pawned his watch in Montgomery for $15; the fourth attempt succeeded; he never saw the watch again | *Up From Slavery*, ch. 10 (Lit2Go / Literature Project texts); LitCharts / Course Hero ch. 10 summaries |
| Students built campus buildings with bricks they made (Alabama Hall 1884, first brick building; Thrasher Hall 1893) | NPS tuin timeline/brochure ("with bricks they made themselves, raised buildings"); NPS "One Brick at a Time: Robert R. Taylor"; SAH Archipedia |
| At Washington's death (1915): about 1,500 students and 100+ buildings | Tuskegee University "History and Mission" (1,500 students, 100 buildings, $2M endowment); Britannica; NPS (1,537 students); Wikipedia ("more than 100 buildings") |

Left out: "age 25" when hired (only weaker sources), the Foster vote deal (accounts vary), 1.2 million chapel bricks (single blog), brick sales to white townspeople (not in the sources found), exact acreage (figures vary by year).
